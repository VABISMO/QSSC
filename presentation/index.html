A room-temperature solid-state spin-defect quantum platform — Qiskit backend, FPGA control electronics, a validated chip fabrication protocol, and a web dashboard. Built by merging an hBN spin-defect chip design with a Qiskit NV-diamond simulator.

01 · The idea

A quantum chip that runs at 300 K

Most quantum hardware needs dilution refrigerators (millikelvin). Solid-state spin defects in wide-bandgap hosts keep quantum coherence at room temperature — they are the basis of this chip. We turn an ensemble of them into a programmable qubit register.

Spin defect = qubit

A localized electronic spin with an addressable microwave transition. The |0⟩/|1⟩ basis is the m_s sublevels of the spin ground state.

Optical readout

Spin state is read through spin-dependent photoluminescence (ODMR) — no cryogenic electronics needed for initialisation or measurement.

Solid-state & scalable

Hosts are hBN, diamond, SiC — 2D/3D crystals compatible with wafer-scale photonics and on-chip detectors.

02 · The chip

A van der Waals heterostructure

  • CQD emitter array — electrically pumped amplified spontaneous emission (ASE) source, not a laser.
  • Polymer waveguides — route excitation light across the defect field.
  • hBN spin-defect layer — the qubit register (V_B⁻ boron-vacancy centres).
  • 2D photodetector array — per-site spin-state readout via ODMR.
  • Peltier + liquid cooling — keeps ΔT stable for coherence, still ~300 K class.

04 · ODMR physics

Reading a spin with light

odmr — hBN V_B⁻, B = 1 mT
  • Spin resonance splits as f = D ± γ·B (Zeeman) plus hyperfine replicas from the host nuclei.
  • At resonance, photoluminescence dips — the spin goes dark. Depth of dip ∝ spin population.
  • The simulator uses a fast analytic Lorentzian model (D ± γB + A·m_i + Q·m_i²) — no heavy QuTiP dependency.
  • XY8 dynamical-decoupling sequences extend T₂; the backend models this as coherence damping.

09 · Fabrication protocol

A complete, validated lab build

Five sections, every step with target parameters and expected outcomes — written so a lab could follow it, and grounded in landmark publications.

1 · CQD emitter array

PEDOT:PSS / CQD / ZnO p-i-n stack, spin-coated, electrically pumped. Honest: ASE, not lasing.

2 · hBN + defects + PMMA

Mechanical exfoliation, V_B⁻ creation (anneal / He implantation), PMMA transfer & encapsulation.

3 · 2D photodetectors

Graphene / MoS₂ CVD growth and transfer for per-site ODMR readout.

4 · Waveguides & assembly

SU-8 lithography, stacking, thermal management (Peltier + liquid).

5 · Safety

Laser Class 3B controls, fume handling, ESD, electrical isolation.

10 · Validation references

Grounded in published science

Gottscholl et al., Nature Physics 16, 1302 (2020) — discovery of the hBN V_B⁻ spin defect with room-temperature ODMR at D ≈ 3.46 GHz. Foundation of the default defect profile.
Hernandez et al., Nature Nanotechnology 3, 315 (2008) — electrical stimulation of single NV centres; basis for the electrically-pumped excitation approach.
Coleman et al., Science 331, 568 (2011) — liquid-exfoliation of 2D materials; underpins hBN flake production.
Baker & Baker (2010) and Kianinia et al., ACS Photonics 7, 2211 (2020) — quantum emitters / defects in hBN; defect-engineering steps.
Chejanovsky et al. — hBN spin-defect single-shot readout work, cited with a verification footnote in the protocol.
