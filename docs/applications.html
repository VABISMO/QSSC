This document outlines where the QSSC platform can be applied, split into its two complementary halves: on-chip quantum sensing (using the NV spin ensemble as a field/temperature/rotation probe) and quantum computation (using the same spins as addressable qubits driven through a Qiskit-compatible control stack). Each capability below is tagged with its real status from the project record — SIM ✅ implemented in the simulator + API, needs-HW requires additional hardware on the PCB, needs-cryo only works cold, and host-side runs in the host control layer.

PART IQuantum sensing applications

The sensing half exploits the NV centre's optically-detected magnetic resonance (ODMR): an applied magnetic field shifts the NV spin resonance, and the shift is read out optically through the 64-channel detector array. Because the same die both drives (emitters) and reads (detectors), the chip functions as a self-contained, array-parallel quantum probe.

1. Vector magnetometry & magnetic-field imaging

SIM ✅

What it does. The 64-pixel vector magnetometer (Invention 1) uses a 4-axis NV⟨111⟩ doublet split to extract a full B-vector per pixel, with gradiometric sign-locking to reject common-mode drift. Readout is array-parallel across all 64 channels.

Geology & archaeology

Locate buried structures, ore bodies and demagnetised artefacts from their weak ambient field anomalies.

Passive magnetic survey

Non-destructive testing

Map crack-induced flux leakage and weld integrity in pipelines, airframes and pressure vessels.

Industrial inspection

Biomedical imaging

Detect magnetised cell labels and neural/ cardiac field signatures with a room-temperature, array-sized aperture.

Life sciences

Materials characterisation

Resolve domain structure and permeability maps in soft magnets, thin films and 2-D materials.

2. Tensor-gradient magnetic imaging & dipole localisation

SIM ✅

What it does. Invention 19 combines the 4-axis readout with the on-board field-gradient coil (now a real PCB feature) to measure the per-pixel B-gradient tensor, then inverts it to localise a magnetic dipole source in 3-D position. The gradient — the same mechanism that tunes the register width — is repurposed as a spatial-encoding tool.

  • Source localisation: pin-point the depth and lateral position of a buried or embedded dipole (unexploded ordnance, structural rebar, current-carrying traces).
  • Tensor gradiometry: reject distant background by measuring the field gradient rather than the field itself — the standard trick for working near magnetic clutter.
  • Cross-correlated with the 64 pixels: one acquisition yields a tensor image, not a single scalar.

3. Navigation & geomagnetic sensing

SIM ✅

What it does. The compass endpoint fits the ODMR doublet to return an ambient B-vector, heading, elevation and sensitivity in µT; the nav endpoint turns ferromagnetic/geomagnetic signatures into a navigation aid. Because the sensor is a chip, it can be embedded where a bulky magnetometer cannot.

  • Denied-environment navigation: GPS-independent heading from the Earth's field.
  • Geomagnetic anomaly navigation: match local field features against a reference map.
  • Current-direction sensing: infer conductor orientation from the field it produces.

4. Rotation / gyro sensing

SIM ✅

What it does. The gyro endpoint runs a Ramsey π/2–T–π/2 sequence; the free-precession phase over the window T yields a rotation rate in °/s with an associated noise figure — a solid-state alternative to a mechanical or ring-laser gyro, derived from the same qubit control stack as the magnetometer.

5. Thermal & electric-current sensing

SIM ✅

What it does. NV resonance is temperature-sensitive, and the on-chip sensor suite exposes thermal and current endpoints alongside ferro (ferromagnetic) imaging:

  • Thermal mapping: measure local temperature from the resonance shift — a quantum thermometer with no cryogenics.
  • Contactless current sensing: infer conductor current from the field it produces, galvanically isolated by construction.
  • Integrated multi-modal probe: field, temperature and current from a single die in a single acquisition stack.

6. Super-resolution & sub-pixel readout

SIM ✅ needs-HW

What it does. Two innovations push spatial information beyond the naive pixel pitch:

  • Sub-pixel centroiding (Invention 6): charge-sharing on the graphene detection sheet yields 64·S spatial channels — extra resolution without extra qubits.
  • Compressed-sensing super-resolution ODMR (Invention 13): OMP sparse recovery over the gradient-bin dictionary deconvolves overlapping resonance lines, lowering the SNR bar for resolving closely-spaced features.
  • Bayesian quantum-limited readout (Invention 14): a one-sided, Bonferroni-corrected matched filter extracts marginal lone-NV lines a hard threshold would miss.
  • N-shot lock-in integration (Invention 7): 1/√N averaging raises the measured register width — a pure software gain.

PART IIQuantum computation applications

The computation half treats the same NV spins as addressable qubits. A Qiskit- compatible control stack compiles circuits into the 19 firmware verbs (emitter drive, MW pulses, readout, gradient, sequences), and the FPGA executes them across the pixel array.

1. Quantum circuit execution (Qiskit)

SIM ✅

What it does. Users submit circuits as OpenQASM 2 or a restricted Python snippet; the builder runs them in a sandboxed namespace (imports limited to qiskit/numpy/math, no file/network/process access) and the control stack executes them. Built-in algorithms already run end-to-end:

Entanglement & benchmarks

Bell, GHZ and QFT states for verification, calibration and teaching.

Validation

Search & optimisation

Grover amplitude amplification and period finding as canonical demonstrations.

Algorithms

Arithmetic

Reversible math operations on encoded registers for algorithmic building blocks.

Subroutines

Custom circuits

User-supplied OpenQASM/Python, up to 64 qubits, for research-specific experiments.

Extensibility
Application mapping: circuit execution is the substrate for chemistry simulation, combinatorial optimisation and machine-learning kernels — the QSSC provides a real, room-temperature execution target rather than a simulator-only workflow.

2. 64-parallel distributed computing

SIM ✅

What it does. Invention 4 exploits the array as 64 independent ~10-NV pixels, delivering 64× throughput — deliberately not one large entangled circuit. The run-parallel API fans a workload across all pixels, and calibrate-array performs a per-pixel array calibration first so each pixel runs at its own measured register width.

  • Ensemble batch processing: run 64 independent trials/samples simultaneously.
  • Per-pixel calibration: array calibration maps each pixel's register before use.
  • Embarrassingly-parallel workloads: sampling, Monte-Carlo and sweep-style jobs scale with pixel count rather than circuit depth.

3. Quantum RNG & QKD

SIM ✅

What it does. Two security applications read straight off the physical entropy of the device:

  • Quantum RNG → Ed25519 keys: harvest READ_RNG entropy from the AD7616 detector noise floor of a dark channel, then derive an Ed25519 keypair and self-signed X.509 certificate from it — hardware-rooted, quantum-safe key generation.
  • BB84 QKD: implement the BB84 protocol with a simulated intercept-resend Eve, demonstrating the quantum-key-distribution workflow end-to-end.
Application mapping: quantum-safe cryptography (keys that cannot be reproduced from a deterministic CSPRNG) and secure key distribution for long-term data protection.

4. Qubit resources: registers & nuclear-spin memory

SIM ✅ needs-HW

What it does. The computation side is built on registers whose width is a measured, tunable quantity:

  • Tunable register width (Invention 5): the on-board field gradient makes the per-pixel register width a measured value — the gradient coil + DAC are on the PCB.
  • Nuclear-spin memory (Invention 3): a ¹³C memory qubit doubles the register via SWAP, giving a longer-lived store than the NV electron spin.
  • Prime-coprime multiplexed readout (Invention 2+): 16 prime-period lock-in channels, exactly orthogonal, with zero cross-talk — scales how many lines can be read at once.
  • Nuclear registers (Inventions 10/11, candidates): ¹³C cluster and NV nitrogen (¹⁴N/¹⁵N) registers for larger qubit resources.

5. Hybrid algorithms & QEC (candidates)

host-side needs-cryo

What it does. Documented candidates that pair the on-chip qubits with a host-side classical loop or push into regimes needing extra hardware:

  • Parallel VQE / QAOA (Invention 15): classical-quantum optimisation loop, host-side.
  • Quantum kernel estimation (QSVM) (Invention 16): quantum feature maps for machine-learning classification, host-side.
  • Small QEC code on the register (Invention 18): an error-correcting code run on the register (needs-HW + host-side).
  • Distributed / Heisenberg-limited sensing (Invention 17): entanglement-enhanced sensing across pixels (needs-cryo; the magnon YIG bus, Invention 8, is likewise cryogenic).
  • Resonant spin-to-charge readout (Invention 12): single-shot readout (needs-HW).

§Why these applications are practical here

Room temperature

300 K operation — no dilution refrigerator. The filter stack (servo/iso/shield/feed-forward/CM/DD) protects SNR and extends T₂ at ambient.

Deployment

Single-chip integration

Drive, MW, readout, gradient and control live on two PCBs around one die — one Artix-7 FPGA runs the whole loop.

Footprint

Array-parallel

64 independent pixels scale throughput linearly instead of relying on one deep entangled circuit.

Throughput

Open source

Boards, RTL, firmware, simulator, API and docs are published and reproducible — auditable end to end.

Accessibility

Qiskit-compatible

Standard OpenQASM/Python circuits lower the barrier from algorithm research to physical execution.

Workflow

Self-verifying

The project's own gates keep the boards, docs and firmware mutually consistent — every claim is checked against measurement.

Rigor
Summary. The QSSC platform addresses two application families from one device: sensing — magnetometry, field/tensor imaging, navigation, rotation, thermal and current probing at room temperature with array parallelism — and quantum computation — Qiskit circuit execution, 64-way distributed processing, quantum-safe key generation and QKD, backed by tunable registers and nuclear-spin memory. Capabilities marked SIM ✅ run today against the published boards; those marked needs-HW, host-side or needs-cryo define the documented roadmap.
